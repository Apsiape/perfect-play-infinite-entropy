\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
The finite game forces a normal pure factor with a critical Catalan tail. At word size $n$ there are $\Cat_n$ Schmidt probabilities equal to $4^{-n}/2$. Their total mass is of order $n^{-3/2}$, while their entropy contribution is of order $n^{-1/2}$. The masses sum to one and the entropy diverges. A finite auxiliary system implements the test but does not cause the divergence. Forthcoming work gives a second finite game, built from a context-free grammar with critically weighted counts of order $n^{-5/4}$, whose canonical entropy exponent is $1/3$ instead of $1/5$, so the canonical exponent is not universal.

\paragraph{The $I_{3322}$ contrast.}
Coladangelo's attaining state \cite[Definition 10.12 and Theorem 10.14]{I3322} has finite entropy. This is a consequence of its construction, not an entropy claim made in that paper. Here is the tail argument. Its Schmidt vector $\lambda$ satisfies a bounded Jacobi equation $J_\infty(c)\lambda=q_*\lambda$, with monotone labels of total variation at most two and $q_*>1/4$. The half-line version of the estimate in its Lemma 10.10 bounds the tail restriction by $1/4+\mathrm{TV}_N/2$. Choose $N$ so this lies below $q_*$. The resolvent equation on that half-line has only a boundary source. The Combes--Thomas exponential-weight estimate \cite{CT}, using bounded off-diagonal entries, gives $\sum_ie^{\mu|i|}\lambda_i^2<\infty$ for some $\mu>0$, on both tails. Gibbs' inequality then gives $H((\lambda_i^2)_i)<\infty$. Thus dimension nonattainment need not force entropy divergence. We do not need a claim about every $I_{3322}$ optimum.

The main open quantitative problem is a lower entropy rate for all near-optimal strategies. The present compactness proof establishes divergence, whereas the spectral calculation gives only a restricted matching power. Smaller question and answer sets are also desirable. Our graph and row constructions leave substantial room for sharing contexts, but any reduction must preserve the same actual products and calibrated transports.

The theorem concerns normal states on tensor products. We do not classify nonnormal states or nonspatial commuting strategies. Nor do we claim dimension-independent robust self-testing, minimal alphabets, or an optimal unrestricted upper constant. These boundaries leave the distinction among dimension, entropy and type III structure visible.
