\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}
The accompanying \texttt{verification/} directory contains both archives, two independent Bell evaluators, two game evaluators, the literal verifier checks, scalar near-optimality checks, recorded outputs and fresh execution logs. Its README gives commands, dependencies, provenance and limitations. \texttt{SHA256SUMS} identifies every bundled file apart from itself. Paths in public files are relative.

The Bell producer-side evaluator reads every literal coefficient and uses exact arithmetic in $\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$. The review evaluator reconstructs the measurements independently in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{24})$, using a different field representation. Both give
\[
 B_0=-2/75,\qquad B_1=-2/375,\qquad B_2=-2/825.
\]
Each finds only the isometry row failing, the predicted full boundary matrix, and zero direct FMS/refinement loss.

The producer-side game checker reuses the producer-side Bell evaluator's matrix kernel. It is independent of the game producer, but its old measurements are not a second independent implementation. The game review supplies that implementation: it independently rebuilds every old projection, FMS/refined measurement and forbidden event, then reconstructs the new gadgets from predicate data. Independent brute-force and changed-column controls accompany the reduced game evaluator. The checks evaluate rule classes and exact structural zeros, not a dense enumeration of every answer pair in the full question square.

The scalar checker audits the literal direct-event classes, performs thirty-two exact rank-one contractions, checks rational taper losses, and evaluates positive scalar sums through $N=10^6$. A second engine for \eqref{eq:filteredloss} contracts all $73\,769$ literal coefficients on filtered states at word cutoffs $M=1,2$, including the tapers and mixed-sign rational filters. It reuses the cross-checked Bell measurement builder; its state construction, contraction, aggregation and comparison are separate. The scripts and recorded and fresh outputs are bundled with hashes. Floating-point asymptotic illustrations do not replace the analytic limits or provide an unrestricted entropy lower rate.
